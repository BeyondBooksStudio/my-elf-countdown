# Visual direction — My Elf Countdown

## Reference interpretation

Use the user's attached Christmas-planner video as high-level inspiration: a cheerful helper elf, a personalized child heading, a visible calendar board, warm festive colors, and golden stars used to celebrate good deeds. Build an original character and interface; do not reproduce the reference's exact elf, layout, artwork, or any third-party trade dress.

## Chosen direction

A cozy, tactile Christmas storybook desk: polished 3D-like illustration with soft rounded forms and warm ambient light, balanced with crisp, accessible digital planner cards. The interface should feel like a treasured paper countdown brought to life, not a loud game.

- **Palette:** Evergreen `#174A3B` for structure and navigation; warm parchment `#FFF9ED` and soft cream for surfaces; cranberry `#9D3340` for restrained festive accents; brass/goldenrod `#D9A83F` for stars and reward highlights; muted winter blue `#B8D8E8` for occasional cool contrast; deep ink `#273630` for readable text.
- **Texture and materials:** Smooth, lightly textured paper, fine ruled lines, soft felt/knit details in the illustration, quiet wood warmth. Keep actual text areas plain and high-contrast.
- **Character:** Original tiny winter helper with rounded cheeks, large warm brown eyes, chestnut curls, a forest-green coat with a simple cream collar, one small brass bell, and a teal knitted cap with a soft pom. Friendly and mischievous in a gentle way; no brand-specific costume or recognizable character silhouette.
- **Composition:** A wide welcome illustration places the helper and standing planner board on the right, leaving visual breathing room on the left. Do not bake words into the art. Keep crops robust for responsive hero use.
- **Icon:** Flat evergreen planner-page silhouette with a single gold star and a small cap-shaped accent, full-bleed square, no text or outer badge.
- **UI motifs:** Paper card tabs, stitched/ruled separators, rounded star tokens, cream panels on an evergreen desktop shell.
- **Motion and access:** One brief star-pop when a parent marks progress; visible focus states, semantic labels, good contrast, keyboard access, and reduced-motion support.

## Voice

Warm, short, and never judgmental. Prefer “You tried it together” and “A little kindness counts” over streaks, scores, or “good/bad” labels. Parent-only settings use calm, direct language. Child view uses short sentences and a clear “Your grown-up saved a surprise” state until a reward is approved.
